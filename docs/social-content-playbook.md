# Social content playbook

Research into what performs for an engineering company in Layrus Labs' niche,
across LinkedIn, X, Instagram, Facebook, TikTok, Shorts and YouTube, with notes
on Nigerian audience behaviour.

Prepared October 2026. Sources are listed at the end; the Nigeria-specific
figures are qualitative playbooks rather than hard benchmarks.

---

## The short version

The same post does not travel across platforms, and the biggest lever is not
ideas — it is repurposing one shoot into six formats.

- **LinkedIn** carries credibility and pipeline.
- **X** carries technical credibility, the diaspora and hiring.
- **Instagram** carries brand reach and top-of-funnel attention.
- **Facebook** carries local discovery and a WhatsApp-adjacent audience.
- **TikTok and Shorts** carry raw reach.
- **YouTube** carries durable search traffic.

---

## What each platform rewards

| Platform | Its job for Layrus | Formats that work | Tone | Cadence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **LinkedIn** | Credibility and pipeline | Technical teardowns, failure post-mortems, one real number from a project, architecture decisions. 800–2,000 chars, no link in the first paragraph | Candid, specific, no corporate voice | 3–5/week, Tue–Thu 8–10am |
| **X** | Tech credibility, diaspora, hiring | Build logs with numbers, board and code photos, threads, contrarian takes. Links are penalised; replies are rewarded | Terse, technical, in-progress | 5/week, one thread minimum |
| **Instagram** | Brand reach and top of funnel | Reels 30s or less: bench shots, teardown loops, lo-fi talking heads, Pidgin-flavoured workplace humour, sitcom-style series | Human, funny, unpolished | 3–4 Reels/week |
| **Facebook** | Local discovery, WhatsApp-adjacent audience | Photos, short clips, community updates, local-language captions. Cross-post from Instagram | Plain, warm | 3/week |
| **TikTok / Shorts** | Raw reach | 15–30s teardown or satisfying build; 45–90s explainer. Shorts is a search library, TikTok is a spike engine | Fast, no intro | Cross-post from Reels |
| **YouTube long** | Durable search | Teardowns and failure analysis — they age for years, unlike benchmarks | Explainer | 1–2/month |

### Evidence behind the table

- Personal LinkedIn profiles reach roughly **561% more people than company
  pages**, so the founder posts and the company page amplifies.
- B2B LinkedIn: failure and lessons-learned posts outperform success stories by
  roughly **3:1**. First-hour engagement is decisive.
- On X, build-in-public updates outperform tutorials and hot takes, and posts
  containing links are suppressed by the ranking.
- Instagram: video gets around **8× the engagement of static**, Reels beat
  carousels, and the three-second hook decides the outcome.
- Hardware teardown content holds median views for years; the outliers are
  spec failures and "impossible" repairs.
- Shorts is search-indexed and compounds over months. TikTok spikes faster but
  starts every video from zero.

## Five pillars that fit the existing work

Repeatable formats, not daily invention.

1. **Teardown.** Open a board, a gateway, a dead sensor. Explain what failed and
   why. The highest-converting B2B format on LinkedIn and the best-performing
   niche on Shorts and TikTok.
2. **The real number.** One specific engineering fact from real work — the
   80/20 escrow computed in integer kobo so the split always reconciles; a
   gateway still reporting after eight months in the field. No adjectives.
3. **Build log.** What was done this week, honestly, including what broke. On X
   and LinkedIn.
4. **Teaching.** "What a gateway actually does, in 60 seconds." Searchable,
   durable, and it shortens the sales conversation.
5. **People and humour.** The bench at 1am, solder smoke, the seventh attempt.
   On Instagram and TikTok this is the whole game — but humour has to hang on a
   product truth. "Client asked for a simple site, then sent 14 more features"
   works because it lands on why discovery exists.

## One shoot, six posts

Film once a week at the bench, about 20 minutes, phone on a tripod, natural
light.

- **Long cut (5–8 min)** → YouTube: the full teardown.
- **30–45s cut** → Instagram Reel, TikTok, Shorts: one surprising moment.
- **Stills** → LinkedIn teardown post, X thread images.
- **Three lines of text** → LinkedIn post: the lesson from the same job.
- **Carousel** → Instagram, Facebook: five slides, one idea each.

Six pieces from roughly one hour. That is how a company this size posts four
times a week without a content team.

## Nigeria-specific notes

- **WhatsApp is where deals happen.** Every caption should end somewhere that
  leads to `wa.me/2349029733354`, not just "link in bio".
- **Pidgin and local language lift reach on Instagram and TikTok**, but keep
  LinkedIn and X in plain English, where the audience is different.
- **Data cost is real.** Keep Reels under 30 seconds, compress hard, and never
  post a three-minute video to a Nigerian mobile feed.
- **Instagram skews older and higher-income than TikTok in African markets**,
  which makes it the better fit for B2B-adjacent work. TikTok is reach, not
  pipeline.

---

## What measurably fails

- "Excited to announce" posts and product-update screenshots.
- Motivational quotes and engagement bait ("Thoughts?").
- A link in every tweet.
- Engagement pods and follow-for-follow.
- Content that has been through a brand-review polish.

The consistent finding: **specific and imperfect beats polished and vague.**

## The first 30 days

- **Week 1:** set up the accounts properly, agree the five pillars, pick two
  shooting slots.
- **Week 2:** one bench shoot, publish the teardown in all formats. Expect
  almost no reach; that is normal.
- **Week 3:** templates — a before/after of the restyle is a natural teardown
  for a second audience.
- **Week 4:** the first "real number" post, and the first Pidgin reel.

Consistent results on X are measured in **12–24 months** at 3–5 posts a week,
not weeks.

# Part two — the LinkedIn checklist, and a video series

The research above covers what performs. This part is the operating checklist
and the format catalogue for a technical video series aimed at developers.

## The LinkedIn checklist

Four things, in the order they matter.

### Content strategy

- **Original, not reshares.** Resharing someone else's post borrows attention
  for an hour; original work compounds.
- **Show unique data, expertise or product detail.** The posts that travel are
  the ones only Layrus could have written.
- **Date the content.** "November 2026" on a chart or a claim signals it is
  current, and dated material is what gets cited later.

### Author and profile strategy

- **Post 2–3× a week.** Three is the ceiling that stays sustainable; one is a
  hobby.
- **Keep the profile public** and grow the follower count — the profile is the
  landing page every post links to.
- **Experts post first, the company amplifies.** Personal profiles reach far
  more people than company pages, so John posts and the page shares.

### Formatting for AI

- **Clear, quotable sentences.** An answer engine quoting one line should get a
  complete thought.
- **Question: Answer format.** It mirrors how people search, and how a model
  retrieves.
- **First line is the takeaway.** Everything else is support.
- **Include images.** Posts with a diagram or screenshot are cited more often
  than text alone.

### Engagement

- **Ask for comments** — with a real question, not "thoughts?".
- **Use video for organic traction.** It is still the format the feed pushes.
- **Boost the posts that matter.** A small spend on the one post a month that
  carries the positioning beats spreading it across ten.

## The video format catalogue

Thirteen formats, and the honest case for each.

| Format | What it is | Best for | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|
| **UI / motion-graphics explainer** | Clean animated UI, diagrams and charts, usually with a 2D character and highlighted captions | Algorithms and system concepts — the format in the reference clip | Medium |
| **Kinetic typography** | Animated text and numbers synced to voiceover, minimal illustration | Definitions, statistics, one-line takes | Low |
| **Blackboard / whiteboard** | Hand-drawn or self-drawing illustrations synced to narration | Foundations: what a queue is, what idempotency means | Low |
| **2D character animation** | Illustrated characters acting out the concept | Series identity, humour, retention across episodes | Medium |
| **Isometric / infographic** | Angled vector systems, animated data charts | Architecture, data flow, before-and-after topology | Medium |
| **Screencast** | Screen recording with cursor zooms and narration | Real code, real product, real debugging | Low |
| **Talking head + b-roll** | A person to camera, cutting to screenshots or footage | Founder point of view, credibility, opinions | Low |
| **Narrated slide deck** | Slides or tablet annotations with voiceover | Deep dives, course-style teaching | Low |
| **3D animation** | Depth-rich models | Embedded and hardware explanations, physical devices | High |
| **Cut-out / collage** | Layered paper-style cutouts and textures | Essay-style commentary, industry takes | Medium |
| **Lightboard** | Presenter behind illuminated glass, writing toward camera | Algorithms and maths, where the working matters | High |
| **AI avatar** | Generated presenter lip-syncing a script | Volume, multilingual versions | Low, with caveats |
| **Photorealistic AI cinema** | Generated live-action scenes with actors and camera movement | A hook for an abstract problem; not for explanation | High |

**Two cautions.** AI avatars and AI cinema cost little to generate and are
instantly recognisable as AI, which undercuts the credibility the series is
built on — use them for the first three seconds at most, or not at all. And the
explainer format only works if the diagram is correct; a wrong arrow teaches
the wrong thing at scale.

## The reference clip, measured

The example runs **60 seconds, 360×640, 30 fps, H.264 with AAC** — vertical,
phone-first, with a 2D character standing beside animated UI cards, a persistent
header (`SYSTEM DESIGN 101`, chapter, follower count), and captions where the
key word is highlighted.

Rebuild it at **1080×1920**. The structure that makes it work:

1. **0–3s** — the naive assumption, stated as a question.
2. **3–20s** — the simple solution, shown as a UI or diagram.
3. **20–40s** — where it breaks, with the failure named on screen.
4. **40–60s** — the fix, its name, and one line on when to reach for it.

One concept per video. The name of the concept appears once, at the end, after
the viewer has felt the problem — naming it first turns a story into a glossary
entry.

## Picking a topic on any day

There is no schedule. The list is a pool: on any day, pick the topic that fits
the week's work or the question someone actually asked, then pick the format
that suits that topic rather than reusing the last one.

| Topic | Format | Why that format |
|---|---|---|
| Caching | UI / motion-graphics | The miss-then-hit path is a diagram, and the stale-price failure needs to be seen |
| Rate limiting | UI / motion-graphics | A counter climbing and requests dropping is motion, not prose |
| Load balancing | Isometric / infographic | It is a topology problem; the shape of the fan-out is the explanation |
| Reverse proxies | Whiteboard | Boxes and arrows in front of one server; drawing it while saying it is the point |
| API gateways | UI / motion-graphics | Compare a proxy with a gateway on the same diagram |
| CI/CD | Screencast | The evidence is a real pipeline running |
| Docker | Screencast | A Dockerfile and a terminal, not an animation of a whale |
| Kubernetes | Isometric / infographic | Pods, nodes and replicas need spatial arrangement |
| Service discovery | Isometric / infographic | Who-knows-who is a graph |
| Timeouts | Kinetic typography | One request, one clock, nothing else on screen |
| Retries | UI / motion-graphics | Three attempts failing in sequence |
| Exponential backoff | Kinetic typography | The numbers themselves are the idea: 1, 2, 4, 8, plus jitter |
| Circuit breakers | UI / motion-graphics | Three states — closed, open, half-open — shown changing |
| Idempotency | Kinetic typography or whiteboard | A duplicated payment is the whole story; keep the frame bare |
| Message queues | Isometric / infographic | Producers, the queue, consumers |
| Pub/Sub | Isometric / infographic | One event fanning to many listeners |
| Event-driven architecture | Whiteboard | It is an argument about how to model a system, not a diagram to admire |
| Distributed transactions | 2D character or whiteboard | The two-generals problem lands better as a story than as boxes |
| Sagas | UI / motion-graphics | A sequence of steps with compensating steps, shown side by side |

**Two rules that hold whatever you pick.** One concept per video, and the same
first three seconds every time — a recognisable opening is what turns separate
videos into a series. Everything else can vary.

### Picking, on any day

Three questions, in order:

1. **What did someone ask this week?** A real question beats a planned topic.
2. **Which shelf does it sit on?** That fixes the length and the format before
   you write anything.
3. **What is the worked example?** If you cannot name one in a sentence, the
   topic is not ready — pick a different one.

## Hooks that hold

The first three seconds are where the video is won or lost, and the evidence is
consistent across platforms.

- Roughly **half of viewers leave before the 3-second mark**, and on TikTok
  about **71% decide to scroll within 3 seconds**.
- Around **65% finish a video when the first five seconds land**.
- Most short-form is watched **without sound** — about 85% — so the hook has to
  exist as on-screen text, not only as a voice.
- The opening has to **pay off the title and thumbnail**. A hook that promises
  something the next ten seconds do not deliver loses the viewer faster than a
  boring opening would.

### Hooks are visual, not textual

A hook is something the viewer **sees** in the first three seconds. Text can
support it; text cannot be it. A sentence on screen asks someone to stop
scrolling and read, which is a second decision after the first one they are
already making — and most of them make it before they have read anything.

The line in the hook bank is the voiceover that lands **after** the shot. The
shot is the hook.

Eight visual hooks that fit technical content:

| Visual hook | Shape | Fits |
|---|---|---|
| **Repetition wall** | The same thing duplicated until it fills the frame | Caching, polling, duplicate work |
| **The break** | Something working, then visibly not | Retries, timeouts, circuit breakers |
| **The wrong number** | One value on screen that contradicts another | Stale cache, clock skew, rounding |
| **The flip** | A diagram turned over to show the hidden half | Reverse proxies, API gateways |
| **The leak** | Something escaping a boundary it should not | Prompt injection, missing auth |
| **The queue** | Items piling up faster than they leave | Backpressure, message queues |
| **The meltdown** | A gauge or counter running past its limit | Rate limiting, load, capacity |
| **The before/after** | One cut between two states of the same thing | Docker, deploys, migrations |

### The five formulas that work for technical content

| Formula | Shape | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| **Contradiction** | "Most people think X. Here is what actually happens." | Creates a gap the viewer needs closed |
| **Cost** | "This one line costs you 40 database queries a second." | Specific, and the number is checkable |
| **Failure first** | "This worked for eight months. Then it started lying." | A story with a wound in it |
| **Specific question** | "Why does the first request after a deploy take 3 seconds?" | A question with stakes beats "want to learn about X?" |
| **Pattern interrupt** | Open on the broken thing, before any words | The visual stops the scroll; the voice explains after |

### The hook bank

Each line is the voiceover that follows the opening shot. Build the shot first;
the line explains what the viewer has already seen.

| Topic | First line (after the shot) | Formula |
|---|---|---|
| Caching | "Your app asks the database the same question a thousand times a day. The answer has not changed." | Cost |
| Rate limiting | "One script, no limit, and your database is gone by morning." | Failure |
| Load balancing | "One server is fine until the day it is not." | Contradiction |
| Reverse proxy | "Everything your users request goes through one box you never think about." | Pattern interrupt |
| Idempotency | "The customer tapped Pay twice. You charged them twice." | Failure |
| Retries | "You added a retry. You doubled your outage." | Contradiction |
| Exponential backoff | "Everyone retried at the same second. That is why it stayed down." | Contradiction |
| Timeouts | "The request never failed. It just never finished." | Contradiction |
| Circuit breaker | "The fastest way to recover is to stop trying." | Contradiction |
| JWT | "You cannot log someone out of a JWT. That is the trade." | Cost |
| CAP theorem | "You cannot pick two. Partition tolerance is not optional." | Contradiction |
| Docker | "It works on your machine. That is the problem." | Contradiction |
| Microcontroller | "Your laptop and this chip are not the same kind of computer." | Contradiction |
| Pull-up resistors | "The button is not broken. The pin is floating." | Failure |
| Kafka vs RabbitMQ | "One of these remembers. The other forgets." | Contradiction |
| RAG | "The model did not lie. It was never given the right page." | Contradiction |
| Fine-tuning | "Fine-tuning will not teach your model a fact. Prompting might not either." | Contradiction |
| Prompt injection | "Your assistant will follow an instruction hidden in a customer's message." | Failure |

**Four rules for using them.**

1. **The hook must be true.** Every line above is a claim the video has to
   support. A contradiction that turns out to be a simplification is how a
   teaching channel loses engineers.
2. **Pay it off inside twenty seconds.** The hook buys attention; the first
   explanation has to start cashing it immediately.
3. **One visual change in the first three seconds.** A cut, a counter starting,
   a diagram appearing — anything that is not the same static frame the viewer
   scrolled past.
4. **Same rhythm every episode.** The words change; the shape of the opening
   does not. That is what makes a series recognisable.

## Accuracy: the traps

Every script gets checked against a primary source before it is filmed —
vendor documentation, an RFC, or the specification itself. These are the topics
where the popular explanation is wrong often enough to be a liability.

| Topic | The version people repeat | What to say instead |
|---|---|---|
| API vs SDK | "An SDK is a kind of API" | An API is the contract; an SDK is a toolkit that usually wraps one |
| Framework vs library | "They are the same thing" | Inversion of control: the framework calls your code, you call the library |
| Latency vs throughput | "They are opposites" | They are different measures; both can improve at once, and both can degrade together |
| CAP theorem | "Pick any two" | During a partition you choose consistency or availability; partition tolerance is not a choice. PACELC extends it: otherwise, latency vs consistency |
| ACID | "It means the database is safe" | Atomicity, consistency, isolation, durability — each is a separate guarantee with its own cost |
| Idempotency | "The same response every time" | The same effect on the system; the response may differ |
| Bloom filter | "It can be wrong either way" | No false negatives, possible false positives — that asymmetry is the point |
| Exponential backoff | "Just wait longer" | Wait longer, and add jitter, or every client retries in lockstep |
| JWT | "It is stateless, so it is better" | Stateless verification is the trade; revocation needs a denylist or a short lifetime |
| HTTP/3 | "It is HTTP/2 but faster" | It runs over QUIC on UDP, which removes TCP head-of-line blocking |
| Containers vs VMs | "Containers are lightweight VMs" | Containers share the host kernel; a VM has its own |
| Serverless | "It scales infinitely" | It scales per request, with cold starts and execution limits |
| Kafka vs RabbitMQ | "They are both message queues" | Kafka is a retained log with consumer positions; RabbitMQ is a broker that hands out and acknowledges messages |
| MQTT QoS 2 | "Exactly once, guaranteed" | Exactly once within the protocol; end-to-end duplicates are still your problem unless the handler is idempotent |
| RAG | "It stops hallucinations" | It grounds answers in retrieved text; retrieval quality decides whether that works |
| Fine-tuning | "It teaches the model your data" | It shapes behaviour and format; it is unreliable for facts, which is what retrieval is for |
| Microcontroller | "A small computer" | A CPU, memory and peripherals on one chip, usually with no OS and hard timing constraints |
| Pull-up resistor | "It just keeps the pin high" | It defines the voltage when nothing is driving the line, so the input does not float |

**The rule:** if a script corrects a common belief, it must show the correct
version working, not just assert it. That is the difference between a channel
engineers trust and one they argue with in the comments.

## Sound and music

A bed does not make a video better on its own. One controlled study found music
in narrated instructional video had little measurable effect on retention, and
can make things harder for viewers with less working memory. What music reliably
does is **carry pace**: it tells the viewer how fast this is going and where the
cuts are. That is the job — not emotion, and not filling silence.

So the default is a restrained bed, ducked under speech, and silence where the
silence is doing work.

### Tempo follows narration speed

| Narration pace | Music | Feel |
|---|---|---|
| ~130 words per minute | **70–90 BPM** | Calm, explanatory, definitions |
| ~150 words per minute | **90–110 BPM** | Standard explainer; the house default |
| ~175 words per minute | **110–130 BPM** | Fast takes, promos, montage |

Match the bed to the voice, not to the topic. A calm topic delivered fast still
needs the faster bed, or the two fight.

### What to use, by content type

| Content | Mood | Instrumentation | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A term** | Neutral, clean | Sparse synth pulse, light keys | One element, no build; the definition is the content |
| **An algorithm** | Steady, methodical | Lo-fi beat, soft percussion, no melody lead | Must not compete with the worked example |
| **A technology** | Curious | Ambient pad, gentle arpeggio | Keep the pad under 2 kHz so it does not mask speech |
| **A tool / screencast** | Invisible | Very low ambient, or none | Keyboard and mouse sounds are the sound design here |
| **Embedded / hardware** | Textural, mechanical | Subtle rhythmic clicks, low drone | Real device sounds beat synthetic ones; record the board |
| **AI** | Clean, contemporary | Minimal synth, no sci-fi clichés | No risers, no "robot" motifs, no dramatic drops |
| **Failure story** | Tension, then release | Low drone that opens into a lighter bed | The cut from tension to relief is the story beat |
| **Promotional** | Confident | Full instrumental, light percussion | The only place a build and a drop belong |

### Clarity rules

- **No lyrics, ever**, on a narrated video. Words fight words.
- **Avoid lead instruments in 1.5–3.5 kHz** — that is where speech intelligibility
  lives. If the track has a piano or guitar lead there, pick another track or
  carve it out.
- **High-pass the music** below roughly 150–200 Hz so the bed does not muddy the
  voice.
- **Duck the bed 6–18 dB** under speech, with a 300–450 ms release, or simply set
  the bed **18–25 dB below** the voice. The W3C's guidance for foreground speech
  is at least 20 dB of separation; complex topics want more.
- **Fade 1–2s in and 2–3s out**, and cut on musical phrases rather than mid-bar.
- **Silence is a tool.** Cut the bed dead at the hook, before the first line, or
  under the one number that matters. Nothing focuses attention like removing the
  thing that was filling the room.

### Loudness

| | Target |
|---|---|
| Master (YouTube, Instagram, TikTok) | **−14 LUFS** integrated, **−1 to −1.5 dBTP** |
| Dialogue-led educational | **−16 to −14 LUFS**, 6–12 dB of dynamic range |
| Music alone under voice | roughly **−25 to −20 LUFS** |

### Licensing: the trap that costs money

Platform availability is **not** commercial clearance. Each library is licensed
for one platform and often for non-commercial accounts only.

| Platform | Cleared source | What it does **not** cover |
|---|---|---|
| **TikTok** | Commercial Music Library, for Business accounts | TikTok only — reposting to Reels or Shorts needs separate clearance |
| **Meta (Facebook, Instagram)** | Meta Sound Collection | Meta only; check whether a track is ads-cleared before boosting |
| **YouTube** | Audio Library | YouTube only; Shorts trending audio is not cleared for business accounts |
| **LinkedIn** | No in-app commercial library | You must source music yourself, licensed for commercial use and cross-posting |
| **X** | No commercial library | Nothing here is safe for a business post |

**Two rights need clearing for any commercial video:** the composition (sync)
and the recording (master). "Royalty-free" is a licence type, not unrestricted
use — check territory, term, paid boosting, and what happens after the licence
ends.

**Keep a log.** For every published video: URL, publication date, track title and
ISRC, licence source, and the date you checked. Library terms change, and the log
is what protects you a year later.

### Where to source

- **Free, platform-scoped:** YouTube Audio Library, Meta Sound Collection.
- **Free/cheap, cross-platform:** Uppbeat, Pixabay Music (check each track's terms).
- **Paid, one licence across platforms:** Epidemic Sound, Artlist, Musicbed.
  For a company posting to five platforms, this is usually the cheaper option
  once a takedown or a muted video is counted.

## Two audiences, not one

The November calendar speaks to business owners. This series speaks to
engineers. They do not want the same post, and mixing them teaches the feed to
show each to the wrong people.

Keep them apart: business content on the founder's LinkedIn and Facebook; the
series on LinkedIn video, YouTube Shorts and X, where the audience already
lives. The series builds the credibility that makes the business content
believable, which is the only reason to make it.
