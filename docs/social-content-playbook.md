# Social content playbook

Research into what performs for an engineering company in Layrus Labs' niche,
across LinkedIn, X, Instagram, Facebook, TikTok, Shorts and YouTube, with notes
on Nigerian audience behaviour.

Prepared October 2026. Sources are listed at the end; the Nigeria-specific
figures are qualitative playbooks rather than hard benchmarks.

---

## The short version

The same post does not travel across platforms, and the biggest lever is not
ideas — it is repurposing one shoot into six formats.

- **LinkedIn** carries credibility and pipeline.
- **X** carries technical credibility, the diaspora and hiring.
- **Instagram** carries brand reach and top-of-funnel attention.
- **Facebook** carries local discovery and a WhatsApp-adjacent audience.
- **TikTok and Shorts** carry raw reach.
- **YouTube** carries durable search traffic.

---

## What each platform rewards

| Platform | Its job for Layrus | Formats that work | Tone | Cadence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **LinkedIn** | Credibility and pipeline | Technical teardowns, failure post-mortems, one real number from a project, architecture decisions. 800–2,000 chars, no link in the first paragraph | Candid, specific, no corporate voice | 3–5/week, Tue–Thu 8–10am |
| **X** | Tech credibility, diaspora, hiring | Build logs with numbers, board and code photos, threads, contrarian takes. Links are penalised; replies are rewarded | Terse, technical, in-progress | 5/week, one thread minimum |
| **Instagram** | Brand reach and top of funnel | Reels 30s or less: bench shots, teardown loops, lo-fi talking heads, Pidgin-flavoured workplace humour, sitcom-style series | Human, funny, unpolished | 3–4 Reels/week |
| **Facebook** | Local discovery, WhatsApp-adjacent audience | Photos, short clips, community updates, local-language captions. Cross-post from Instagram | Plain, warm | 3/week |
| **TikTok / Shorts** | Raw reach | 15–30s teardown or satisfying build; 45–90s explainer. Shorts is a search library, TikTok is a spike engine | Fast, no intro | Cross-post from Reels |
| **YouTube long** | Durable search | Teardowns and failure analysis — they age for years, unlike benchmarks | Explainer | 1–2/month |

### Evidence behind the table

- Personal LinkedIn profiles reach roughly **561% more people than company
  pages**, so the founder posts and the company page amplifies.
- B2B LinkedIn: failure and lessons-learned posts outperform success stories by
  roughly **3:1**. First-hour engagement is decisive.
- On X, build-in-public updates outperform tutorials and hot takes, and posts
  containing links are suppressed by the ranking.
- Instagram: video gets around **8× the engagement of static**, Reels beat
  carousels, and the three-second hook decides the outcome.
- Hardware teardown content holds median views for years; the outliers are
  spec failures and "impossible" repairs.
- Shorts is search-indexed and compounds over months. TikTok spikes faster but
  starts every video from zero.

## Five pillars that fit the existing work

Repeatable formats, not daily invention.

1. **Teardown.** Open a board, a gateway, a dead sensor. Explain what failed and
   why. The highest-converting B2B format on LinkedIn and the best-performing
   niche on Shorts and TikTok.
2. **The real number.** One specific engineering fact from real work — the
   80/20 escrow computed in integer kobo so the split always reconciles; a
   gateway still reporting after eight months in the field. No adjectives.
3. **Build log.** What was done this week, honestly, including what broke. On X
   and LinkedIn.
4. **Teaching.** "What a gateway actually does, in 60 seconds." Searchable,
   durable, and it shortens the sales conversation.
5. **People and humour.** The bench at 1am, solder smoke, the seventh attempt.
   On Instagram and TikTok this is the whole game — but humour has to hang on a
   product truth. "Client asked for a simple site, then sent 14 more features"
   works because it lands on why discovery exists.

## One shoot, six posts

Film once a week at the bench, about 20 minutes, phone on a tripod, natural
light.

- **Long cut (5–8 min)** → YouTube: the full teardown.
- **30–45s cut** → Instagram Reel, TikTok, Shorts: one surprising moment.
- **Stills** → LinkedIn teardown post, X thread images.
- **Three lines of text** → LinkedIn post: the lesson from the same job.
- **Carousel** → Instagram, Facebook: five slides, one idea each.

Six pieces from roughly one hour. That is how a company this size posts four
times a week without a content team.

## Nigeria-specific notes

- **WhatsApp is where deals happen.** Every caption should end somewhere that
  leads to `wa.me/message/FSMZDBDIMZCOJ1`, not just "link in bio".
- **Pidgin and local language lift reach on Instagram and TikTok**, but keep
  LinkedIn and X in plain English, where the audience is different.
- **Data cost is real.** Keep Reels under 30 seconds, compress hard, and never
  post a three-minute video to a Nigerian mobile feed.
- **Instagram skews older and higher-income than TikTok in African markets**,
  which makes it the better fit for B2B-adjacent work. TikTok is reach, not
  pipeline.

## What measurably fails

- "Excited to announce" posts and product-update screenshots.
- Motivational quotes and engagement bait ("Thoughts?").
- A link in every tweet.
- Engagement pods and follow-for-follow.
- Content that has been through a brand-review polish.

The consistent finding: **specific and imperfect beats polished and vague.**

## The first 30 days

- **Week 1:** set up the accounts properly, agree the five pillars, pick two
  shooting slots.
- **Week 2:** one bench shoot, publish the teardown in all formats. Expect
  almost no reach; that is normal.
- **Week 3:** templates — a before/after of the restyle is a natural teardown
  for a second audience.
- **Week 4:** the first "real number" post, and the first Pidgin reel.

Consistent results on X are measured in **12–24 months** at 3–5 posts a week,
not weeks.
