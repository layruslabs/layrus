Competitor research

Who Layrus Labs is actually up against

Three markets overlap on this company: Abuja software agencies, Nigerian embedded and IoT firms, and AI-automation shops. Nobody sells all three, which is the opening. Everything below is public — company sites and B2B directories — and directory pricing is self-reported.

Prepared October 2026 · layrus.com

The short version

  • Software agencies stop at the API. They sell web and mobile builds, priced from about $25/hour up to $150, and none of them mention hardware.
  • Embedded firms stop at the gateway. They are hardware-first and sector-locked — grid, climate, agriculture — and sell no software or cloud service line.
  • AI automation is crowded and cheap. WhatsApp agents start around ₦120,000. Competing there on price is unwinnable, and the phrase itself is now a commodity.

The most contested market in Nigerian tech. Entry is around ₦120,000, and several of these firms are younger than two years. "We do AI automation" no longer distinguishes anyone.

Where the gap is

Almost nobody sells the whole stack. Software agencies stop at the API, embedded firms stop at the gateway, automation shops stop at the WhatsApp flow.

Devices, firmware, software, cloud, AI and the support contract, from one team, is a claim none of these competitors makes — and the market prices around it rather than through it. Century floors at $10k, Haqqman charges $70–150/hour, while the automation shops chase ₦120,000 projects. The space between — a ₦2–15m system with hardware and a maintenance agreement — looks thinly served.

Threats worth naming

AI automation price collapse

A ₦120,000 WhatsApp bot undercuts any proposal that mentions AI, whatever the engineering behind it.

The template market

With templates.layrus.com live, Envato and the agencies reselling themes compete for the same small-business budget as entry-level work.

Institutional procurement

For government and bank work, Century's references beat a young company's portfolio regardless of engineering quality.

What this implies

  • Lead with the stack, not the services. "We build the device, the software and the cloud" is defensible; "we do custom software" is not.
  • Publish outcomes, not features. Every competitor lists services; none shows a metric. Two case studies with numbers would separate Layrus faster than any repositioning.
  • Don't compete with Jorion on WhatsApp bots. Sell automation as an extension of a system Layrus built or maintains, priced as part of the platform.

Rates and headcounts come from company sites and directories (Sortlist, Clutch, GoodFirms, Crustdata) and are self-reported. Treat them as signals, not facts, and re-check before quoting any figure in a proposal.
