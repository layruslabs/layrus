# Layrus Labs — 30-Day Social Media Content Calendar

**Period:** 1–30 November 2026
**Market:** Nigeria first, with potential reach across West Africa and the international engineering community
**Primary objective:** Build technical credibility, demonstrate engineering capability, and generate qualified business enquiries.

The calendar follows one central principle: **show the engineering, explain the decisions, and make the business value visible.**

Rather than creating 30 unrelated ideas, we'll build a content system around real engineering work. One project can produce a teardown, a short video, a technical post, a carousel, a build log, and a longer explainer — each adapted to its platform.

The calendar covers Layrus Labs' four service pillars:

- **Software Systems**
- **AI & Automation**
- **Embedded Systems & IoT**
- **Cloud Infrastructure**

It also includes systems integration, maintenance, and practical engineering lessons.

Visual hooks are referred to by number throughout this calendar. All 100 are defined in [visual-hooks.md](./visual-hooks.md).

## Before publishing: the evidence rule

I don't yet have verified project details or performance measurements for every example in this calendar. So I've marked places where you must supply real project evidence.

Use these labels throughout:

- **[REAL PROJECT]** — insert an actual Layrus project, prototype, or client engagement.
- **[VERIFIED METRIC]** — use only a measurement you can substantiate.
- **[DEMO]** — clearly present a controlled demonstration when it isn't a production deployment.
- **[LESSON]** — share an engineering principle without implying that Layrus experienced a specific incident.
- **[CTA]** — adapt the call to action to the post's objective.

Never turn a hypothetical failure into a claimed client incident or a simulated metric into a production result.

## 1. Publishing strategy and test windows

The calendar begins on Sunday, 1 November 2026. The posting times below are in **West Africa Time (WAT, UTC+1)**.

Recent 2026 research shows why we shouldn't treat morning as the universal best time: Buffer's analysis of 4.8 million LinkedIn posts found strong engagement in weekday late-afternoon and evening windows, with Wednesday at 4 p.m. performing particularly well in its dataset. This is a useful comparison point, not a Nigeria-specific guarantee.

We'll test two windows rather than commit prematurely to one.

| Platform | Initial test windows | Main purpose |
|---|---|---|
| LinkedIn | Tue 12 p.m.; Wed 4 p.m.; Thu 9 a.m. | Credibility and qualified enquiries |
| X | Tue 1 p.m.; Thu 6 p.m. | Engineering conversation |
| Instagram | Wed 1 p.m.; Thu 7 p.m. | Visual discovery and profile visits |
| Facebook | Tue 1 p.m.; Thu 7 p.m. | Local visibility and enquiries |
| TikTok | Wed 3 p.m.; Fri 6 p.m. | Discovery and creative testing |
| YouTube Shorts | Wed 1 p.m.; Sat 11 a.m. | Discovery and evergreen technical content |
| YouTube long-form | One Sunday, 4 p.m. | Detailed engineering education |

These are experimental slots. After two weeks, compare actual account results and adjust.

### The weekly operating rhythm

- **Monday:** Publish a technical problem or teardown.
- **Tuesday:** Share a technical insight on X and a business-facing post on LinkedIn.
- **Wednesday:** Publish a visual demonstration and repurpose it for short-form video.
- **Thursday:** Explain an engineering decision or lesson.
- **Friday:** Publish a short demonstration, build log, or relatable engineering moment.
- **Saturday:** Publish selectively; use the remaining time to edit and review.
- **Sunday:** Review performance, engage with relevant people, and prepare the next week's content.

This is a working rhythm, not a requirement to publish on every platform every day.

## 7. How to turn one shoot into six formats

This is the most important production system in the calendar. **Don't film six separate pieces of content when one well-planned shoot can supply the raw material for all six.**

Use Day 4's monitoring demonstration as an example.

| Format | Platform | Content created from the shoot |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Short demonstration | TikTok, Instagram Reels | The dashboard warning, alert, and recovery |
| 2. Technical explanation | LinkedIn | Why monitoring matters after deployment |
| 3. Build log | X | The system behaviour, diagnostic steps, and technical lesson |
| 4. Carousel | Instagram, Facebook | Five stages of monitoring and incident response |
| 5. Short technical explainer | YouTube Shorts | How abnormal metrics can trigger an alert |
| 6. Long-form breakdown | YouTube | Monitoring architecture, alert thresholds, investigation, and recovery |

The content should be adapted to each platform, not merely uploaded unchanged.

### One shoot, six formats: production checklist

During each shoot, capture:

- **Wide shot:** The complete setup.
- **Macro shot:** The most visually interesting detail.
- **Action shot:** A process actually happening.
- **Screen recording:** The software or dashboard responding.
- **Before-and-after:** The original state and the result.
- **Explanation:** A short recording of the engineer explaining the problem and decision.

Film vertical footage for Reels, TikTok, and Shorts. Capture additional horizontal footage if you intend to make a YouTube long-form video.

Keep important visual elements away from the edges so platform interfaces and captions don't obscure them.

For technical demonstrations, capture enough detail to explain what is happening. Don't sacrifice clarity just to make the video shorter.

## 9. Nigeria-specific execution

The calendar should feel like an engineering company operating in Nigeria, not a generic international technology brand with a Nigerian address.

That doesn't mean forcing Nigerian slang into every post.

### Language

Use **plain English as the default** for technical explanations, particularly on LinkedIn and X.

Test natural Nigerian English or Pidgin for selected humorous videos and familiar workplace situations.

For example:

**Plain English version:**

> The requirements changed again. Now the original solution needs a redesign.

**Pidgin-flavoured version:**

> You don finish the work, then dem say, "Abeg, na just small change."

The second version should sound natural when spoken. Don't force Pidgin into a serious technical explanation if it makes the message less clear.

Treat this as a creative experiment, not a claim that Pidgin automatically increases reach.

### Data-conscious production

- Start with short demonstrations when the story allows it.
- Compress videos sensibly.
- Add captions so the content remains understandable without sound.
- Avoid long branded introductions.
- Use longer videos when they provide meaningful technical value.

A 45-second explanation that solves a real problem can be more useful than a 15-second video that says very little.

### WhatsApp conversion

Use WhatsApp when a viewer is ready to discuss a project or request further information.

A relevant closing line might be:

> Have a business process you'd like to simplify? Contact Layrus to discuss the problem and possible approaches.

Link this to the appropriate contact destination.

For educational posts, a follow, save, or visit to a relevant project page may be a more natural next step. Don't force every viewer into a sales conversation.

## 10. How to measure the first 30 days

The first month is an experiment. The goal is to discover which content attracts the right audience, not to prove that every post can go viral.

### Weekly performance dashboard

Track the following once per week:

| Metric | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| Reach and impressions | How widely the content was distributed |
| Three-second retention, where available | Whether the opening captured attention |
| Average watch time | Whether viewers stayed with the video |
| Completion or retention | Whether the content held attention |
| Shares and saves | Whether viewers found the content useful or worth passing on |
| Relevant comments | Whether it attracted people interested in the subject |
| Profile visits | Whether viewers wanted to learn more about Layrus |
| Website visits | Whether content generated further interest |
| WhatsApp enquiries | Whether viewers initiated a conversation |
| Qualified leads | Whether the conversation involved a genuine potential customer |

Instagram's ranking documentation explains that the platform predicts different types of viewer interaction, including watching, liking, saving, and visiting profiles. YouTube similarly uses viewer behaviour and personalisation to recommend content. Neither platform provides a universal threshold that guarantees distribution.

### Review after each week

**Week 1: Establish a baseline.** Record performance without making strong conclusions from small samples.

**Week 2: Compare hooks.** Compare the performance of a visual failure, a macro reveal, a before-and-after transformation, and a satisfying build sequence.

**Week 3: Compare subjects.** Determine whether your audience responds more strongly to software, AI automation, embedded systems, or infrastructure.

**Week 4: Compare audience quality.** Look beyond views. Identify which posts attracted relevant technical conversations, business enquiries, and visits to your service pages.

### What counts as success?

At the end of November, aim to answer five questions:

1. Which visual openings most consistently captured attention?
2. Which subjects attracted the audience Layrus wants to reach?
3. Which platforms generated the most meaningful interactions?
4. Which formats were easiest to produce consistently?
5. Which posts contributed to genuine interest in Layrus' services?

Don't set arbitrary targets such as a particular number of leads or a guaranteed percentage increase before you have a baseline. Set numerical goals after reviewing the first week of data.
